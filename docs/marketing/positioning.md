Working title: One-Man Team. Studio: Ollywood Games. First platform: Google Play. Written 21 November 2026.

## Who it is for

1. **Football-literate adults who play in short gaps.** People who know what a third-man run or a cut-back is and will enjoy doing one with their own thumbs. This is the core audience and the one all store copy should address.
2. **Players who liked the "one decisive move" idea in highlight-style football games but not the waiting.** Pocket Gamer's 2015 review of Score! Hero praised the through-ball moments and criticised the energy and retry economy and the feeling that results were scripted. One-Man Team keeps the moment and removes both complaints: every outcome is the player's own input, and a failed round costs a few seconds.
3. **Puzzle-action players who like playing against their own past.** The Does Not Commute audience: people who enjoy a level that gets harder because of what they did thirty seconds ago.

Not the target: people who want a full match, a squad to manage, real clubs, or online play.

## The one-sentence pitch

A football game where you play every player in one attacking move, one at a time, while defenders react live to all of them.

Shorter, for captions and the icon's neighbour text: **Play the pass. Rewind. Run onto it yourself.**

## Proof points

1. **You are the passer and the runner.** Each level is one move built in rounds. Round one is the pass into space; the clock rewinds; round two is you arriving on it while the first player replays. Up to five players.
2. **Defenders are live, not recorded.** A new run changes what the defence does, so it can open space or ruin a pass that worked two rounds earlier. Decoys who never touch the ball are a real job, and you can be offside against your own pass.
3. **Five to fifteen seconds a level, retry in a breath.** Failure replays only the current player. Three stars for two retries or fewer (this is the rule in the current build).
4. **The replay is the reward.** After the goal the whole move plays back with every run drawn on the pitch. It is a finished, legible ten-second clip of something the player built alone.

A fifth, for press and store art rather than the pitch: it has a look of its own (tabletop figures on felt, matchday-programme menus) and no licences to imitate.

## What it is not

- Not a match simulator. There is no ninety minutes, no opposition attack, no scoreline to manage.
- Not a "draw the play and press go" planner. Every run and pass is performed in real time with two thumbs.
- Not a swipe-to-curl shooting game.
- Not a management game, card collector or online game.
- Not licensed. No real clubs, leagues or players, and the marketing should never imply otherwise.
- Not endless. Around 20 handmade levels at launch. Say so; do not hide it.

### What the comparables tell us

- The scenario format is proven at very large scale, but the incumbent leans on energy and currency, which is exactly what reviewers objected to.
- The rewind-and-replay format is proven as a premium-feeling small game with a one-off unlock and no ads, at roughly ten million Android installs over a decade.
- The one direct football-puzzle neighbour I found is a planner, not a performer: you draw, then watch.

## The gap this game fills

Among the games I could find, none lets the player perform every role in a football move in real time, against defenders who respond to each new run, with earlier selves replaying alongside. Score! Hero gives the moment but scripts the context and meters the retries. Does Not Commute gives the self-versus-self tension but has nothing to do with football. Football Playbook gives the tactics but takes the thumbs away.

The position to hold: **the football move you build yourself, one player at a time, with nothing between you and the retry.** The honest limit on that claim is that I searched; I did not prove absence.

## Risks to the positioning

- **"Football puzzle" undersells the action; "football game" oversells the scope.** Use "a football game about one move" in prose and keep "puzzle" for the store title, where it sets expectations for people who would otherwise want full matches.
- **Twenty levels is thin against a competitor with hundreds.** Position on craft and the replay, never on quantity.
- **The Sports category is owned by publishers with nine-figure install counts.** Discovery will come from the shareable replay and the playable web version, not from category browsing.
