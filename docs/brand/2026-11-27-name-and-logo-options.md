# Name and logo options for the web launch

Drafted 27 November 2026 by the brand team (strategy, naming, identity, games marketing) for the owner to choose from. Nothing here has been applied to the game. The live web build, the store listings and the icons in `one-man-team/icons/` are unchanged.

The brief: launch on the web first so marketing can start without waiting for store review, and draft names and logos. The owner prefers One-Man Team, so it is the baseline. The alternatives are here to test it, not to replace it by default.

## 1. Names

### The baseline: One-Man Team

- **Idea:** you are the whole team, one player at a time.
- **Why it fits:** it is already on the cover, the share text and the web address. People know the phrase. It is short, and it sets up the joke well ("you are the whole team").
- **Risks:** the idiom usually means a side that relies on one star, so on its own it does not explain the rewind. "Man" is gendered in a sport with a large women's audience. As a phrase it is everywhere in sports writing, so web search will not find the game by name alone. People will type it with a hyphen, without one and as one word.

### Alternatives

**Direction 1: the replay mechanic (you pass to the player you were a moment ago)**

| Name | The idea in one line | Why it fits | Risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pass to Yourself | The rule of the game, written as an instruction. | The only name that tells a stranger what they will actually do, and it still sounds like football. | Long (16 characters) for an icon label. It describes the first two rounds of a move better than the fifth. |
| Own Assist | You set up your own goal. A play on "own goal". | Dry and British. A strong, short wordmark. | Means nothing to people who do not follow football. "Own" brings "own goal" to mind, which is a failure. |
| Team of One | One-Man Team without the gendered word. | Closest to the owner's favourite, and gender-neutral. | Business and military writing use the phrase constantly, so it is hard to find in search. Less distinctive. |
| Ghost Run | You run alongside your own ghosts. In football, "ghosting" into the box means arriving late and unmarked. | It joins the mechanic and the football meaning in two words. | Crowded name (see the checks below). |

**Direction 2: football culture and terrace language**

| Name | The idea in one line | Why it fits | Risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Myself United | A football club with one member: you. | It sounds like a real club. It is funny, gender-neutral, and suits a crest, a scarf and terrace chants. | "United" makes most people think of one particular club, which some fans will enjoy and some will not. It does not explain the rewind. |
| Printworks FC | Named after the club in the story, Kettlewick Printworks. | It is unique and ties the matchday-programme look to the story. | Says nothing about the mechanic, and the club name means nothing to a newcomer. |
| One-Two | A one-two (wall pass) normally needs two players. Here you play it alone. | Every fan knows the term, and the game makes a joke of it. | Very common as a name. Practically impossible to own in search or in a store. |

**Direction 3: the tactics board**

| Name | The idea in one line | Why it fits | Risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Overlap | The overlapping run, and your runs overlapping in time. | A real tactical term that also describes the mechanic. | It is the name of a major football media brand (see the checks). Rejected. |
| Run It Back | Sports slang for "again", and literally what the rewind does. | Says replay and retry in three words. | A stock phrase used by many products. Weak in search. |

**Direction 4: short, punchy store names**

| Name | The idea in one line | Why it fits | Risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solo XI | An eleven made of one player. | Short, and it looks good as an icon. | A move uses at most five players, not eleven. Stores are already full of "Eleven" titles. "XI" is hard to say out loud. |

### Checks on the top five and the baseline

Limits: the Play Store and App Store cannot be searched directly from this environment, and registry (RDAP/WHOIS) look-ups were blocked by the network proxy. "Nothing surfaced" therefore means nothing turned up in web search, and domain status is only what search revealed. **This is not legal advice or a trademark clearance.** Before committing to a name, a lawyer should confirm it, at minimum against USPTO, UKIPO, EUIPO and CIPC. Check domain availability at a registrar before announcing.

### Shortlist, ranked

1. **One-Man Team, with "Pass to yourself." as the line under it.** Recommended for the web launch. The name is already live, the owner prefers it, and the one trademark doubt turned out to be a cancelled clothing mark. Its weakness is that the name alone does not explain the mechanic, and "Pass to yourself" fixes exactly that in three words. The gendered reading is a real but modest cost. Keep it in check by making sure the store art and the clips never show only male-coded figures, and by using "you are the whole team" in the copy.
2. **Pass to Yourself.** The clearest and safest name we found, and the best choice if the owner is willing to rename before the stores. It costs one more change across the cover, the share text and the web address, and a longer icon label: it truncates to "Pass to You..." on some home screens (see the context mocks).
3. **Myself United.** The boldest and most shareable: a club you can make a crest, a scarf and a chant for. It suits fan culture better than either of the others, but explains the game least. It would also make a strong name for a season, a mode or the club's merchandise joke if One-Man Team stays.

Own Assist was the closest to making the shortlist. It is witty, but non-fans get nothing from it, and it brings "own goal" to mind.

## 2. Logo concepts

Every concept is a real SVG, and all type is converted to outlines from the house fonts (Anton, Barlow Condensed) with HarfBuzz kerning, so the files render the same anywhere. There are no gradients, stock icons or clip-art footballs. The figures are the game's own tabletop figures seen from above, and the only shading is a flat offset shadow, as in the art direction.

Files are in `docs/brand/concepts/`. Each concept has `-icon.svg` (1024 master, full bleed for store masking), `-wordmark.svg`, `-wordmark-dark.svg`, `-lockup.svg` and `-lockup-dark.svg`.

The figure reads well at 48 px, but the ghosts mostly disappear at that size. The wordmark is the busiest of the nine. |

## 3. Web launch angle (one page)

**One-line pitch:** a free football game in your browser where you play every player in one attacking move, one at a time, while the defenders react to all of you.

**Tagline options**

| One-Man Team | Pass to Yourself | Myself United |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pass to yourself. | Play the pass. Rewind. Run onto it. | Eleven players. All of them you. |
| The whole team. One player at a time. | Nobody else is getting on the end of it. | The only squad that never argues. |
| You played it. You ran onto it. You scored it. | Football's oldest instruction, finally possible. | Founded today. Squad: you. |

**The first five moves (web first, no store needed)**

1. **Make the Daily Move link the product.** The share text already sends `...one-man-team/?daily=N` with the time and number of takes. Lead every post with it. "Daily 12, The Counter, 6.6 s, one take. Your turn:" is a challenge a friend can open with no install. Add a picture to the share (a still "move card" of the replay with the run lines and time, in programme styling) so it does not travel as bare text. In the meantime, post one card a day by hand.
2. **Post one 10-15 second portrait clip a day** to TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels: a pass into empty space, the rewind, the run onto it, then the full replay. The caption is the move's name and the time, nothing more ("The third-man run, 7.4 seconds"). Every third clip is a failure: getting flagged offside against your own pass is the game's best joke.
3. **Post in football-tactics communities with a football question, not an advert.** For example, "Is this a third-man run or a one-two with a decoy?", with the clip and the Daily link in a comment. Read each community's self-promotion rule first.
4. **Do one post each, a few days apart, in browser-game communities** (r/WebGames, r/playmygame, r/indiegames, itch.io). Also list the web build on itch.io as a free browser game with the store badge "coming soon", so it can be found there too.
5. **Tell the Johannesburg story locally.** "One person in Johannesburg made a football game you play in your browser" is the angle for South African football pages, tech writers and gaming groups. Send them the Daily link, one GIF and three sentences. Keep a "Get it on Google Play" slot on the web cover for when the store listing is live.

Not yet: paid ads, a Discord server, a press kit or influencer fees, as in `docs/marketing/launch-plan.md`.
