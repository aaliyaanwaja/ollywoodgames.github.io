# One-Man Team: naming

Written 21 November 2026. I am not a lawyer and this is not a trademark clearance. It is a record of what ordinary web searches did and did not turn up. Before committing to any name, run it through the free official registers (USPTO, UKIPO, EUIPO, and CIPC in South Africa) and, if money is ever spent on the brand, pay for a proper search.

## How the checks were done, and their limits

- Web searches for each name in quotes, with and without "game", "app", "football", "Google Play" and "App Store".
- **Play Store and App Store search could not be queried directly** from this environment. A name that shows "nothing found" below means "nothing surfaced through web search", which is weaker than a search inside the stores. Type each shortlisted name into the Play Store and App Store search boxes on a phone before deciding.
- Trademark searching was limited to what general web search returns from public trademark sites. I could not open individual register records.

## The working title: One-Man Team

| Check | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Clarity | Partly clear. The idiom normally means a team that depends on one star, which is close to the truth but points at a career-hero game rather than at the mechanic. It does not say football, and it does not hint at the rewind. A subtitle has to carry both. |
| Memorability | Good. A familiar phrase, three short words, easy to say and to spell. |
| Searchability | Weak on the open web: the phrase is everyday sports-writing language, so articles will bury the game. Acceptable inside a store, where an exact title match usually surfaces, but untested here. The hyphen invites three spellings (One-Man Team, One Man Team, OneManTeam); use one everywhere and expect people to type the others. |
| Inclusivity | The weak point. "Man" is gendered in a sport with a large women's game and audience. It will not offend most players, but it is an avoidable narrowing, and store art that shows only male-coded figures would compound it. |
| Same or similar names found | No game or app called exactly "One-Man Team" surfaced. Near neighbours: **One Man Football**, a small free mobile-web football game by TeamCarrot on itch.io (closest in both name and subject, tiny in scale); **OneMan / One Man - Reflex Brawler**, a beat-'em-up by solo developer Francis Entereso on iOS and Android (Pocket Gamer, Jan 2020); **One-Man Army: Lone Survivor** on the App Store; **One For Eleven**, a football management game. None is the same name. |
| Trademark | One thing to check: a web search returned a US trademark record titled **ONEMANTEAM** (serial 87283893) on Justia. The page would not open, so I do not know the owner, the goods or whether it is live. Look it up on the USPTO register before going further. I found nothing else, which is not the same as there being nothing. |

**Verdict on the working title:** usable, with a known inclusivity cost and one open trademark question. It is a good name for a different, more conventional game than this one; it describes the fantasy rather than the mechanic.

## Top three

1. **Pass to Yourself.** It is the only candidate that tells a stranger what they will do in the game, and it does so in three plain words that also sound like football. It is gender-neutral, nothing surfaced with the same name, and the phrase is rare enough to own in search. In the store it leaves room for a category word: "Pass to Yourself: Football" is 26 characters. Weaknesses: at 16 characters it is longer than the working title, so "Football Puzzle" will not fit after it; and it names the first two rounds of a move better than the fifth.
2. **Own Assist.** The best name for the football audience specifically: short, dry, and it matches the programme-and-ticket-stub tone of the art direction. It would make a strong icon wordmark. It loses to number one because a non-fan gets nothing from it and because "own" carries the faint smell of "own goal".
3. **Team of One.** The working title with the gendered word removed. Lowest risk of the three in tone, weakest in search and distinctiveness. Choose it only if the owner wants to stay close to the current name.

## Recommendation

Rename to **Pass to Yourself** before the store listing is created, because a package name and title are cheap to change now and awkward later. If the owner prefers to keep **One-Man Team**, that is a defensible choice provided the ONEMANTEAM trademark record is checked first and the store art avoids making the gendered reading worse. Either way, do the in-store searches by hand first; I could not.
