# Playtest 1: product review (21 Nov 2026)

## Recommendation

Build no new mechanics yet. Spend two weeks on four cheap changes (a no-fail practice move, stars that stop punishing retries, a slightly longer clock, an on-device results log), then put the game in front of 12 to 25 people who did not invent it. Position it as hard but fair with instant retries. Park the full pitch; treat control order as a later experiment.

## 1. Reading the signal

It tells us the retry loop has pull and failure is cheap enough that 20 attempts felt like play. That is the hardest thing to design.

It tells us nothing about a stranger. The owner already understands the rewind and has an inventor's patience.

One fact reframes item (a). I ran the repository's jitter test: a bot with perturbed timing and aim passes every round of levels 1-4 at 95-100%. The layouts are already forgiving; the difficulty is in the hands and head (two-thumb control, grasping the rewind), not defender counts.

**The most important unknown:** does a football-literate stranger grasp the rewind within 90 seconds and choose to retry after failing? **Cheapest test:** watch three people play the existing web build in silence. No code needed.

## 2. Sorting the six

| Item | Verdict | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (a) Opening too hard | Fix first | I disagree with "make it much easier": removing defenders has been tried. Add one or two practice moves with no defender, clock or fail state, and smooth the jump to five defenders at level 5. |
| (b) Hard but addictive | No action | The hypothesis to test, not a finding. |
| (c) Stars punish retries | Fix first | The owner is right, and it is an evening's work. Keep stars but change the measure: one for scoring, two and three for beating par times (best time is already stored). Show retries as a neutral count. |
| (d) Clock frustrating | Small fix, then measure | Add about 20% to limits. Whether the frustration is the limit or waiting on the shared timeline is unknown; log clock failures per level and ask. |
| (e) Full pitch, more players | Park | Good idea, wrong time. |
| (f) Vary control order | Experiment later | Cheaper than (e); not in these two weeks. |

## 3. The two mechanic ideas

Day-seven retention is at risk because 20 levels run out, not for lack of variety. A mechanic earns its place only if it makes new levels more varied per evening of work.

**(e) Full pitch.** Would need to be true: testers finish the 20 and ask for more; the four- and five-player levels are liked, not merely survived (their final rounds already fall to 45-70% in the jitter test); figures stay readable at half today's 20-24 px, or a moving camera gets built. Smallest experiment, free: compare quit points and "favourite level" answers for five-player levels 14, 17 and 19 against the rest. If long chains are not favourites, drop it.

**(f) Run before pass.** Would need to be true: players are secure in the basic rewind first (never season one); passing to your own moving ghost feels different, not confusing; and the engine can separate recording order from ball order, which the brief suggests it cannot today. Smallest experiment: one bonus level after level 8, only if engineering sizes it at two evenings or fewer.

## 4. Difficulty as positioning

Recommend **hard but fair, retry in a breath**. Twenty easy levels would be finished in half an hour; difficulty turns them into an evening. Broad accessibility needs hundreds of levels, which a solo developer cannot supply, and the intended audience accepts retries. The risk is poor reviews from people expecting a casual kickabout.

Implications: the opening teaches and never tests; rewards honour mastery, not fewest tries; the listing says "20 handmade moves. You will retry. Retries are instant and free." Positioning.md quotes the old star rule and needs updating.

## 5. Two-week plan (evenings)

- **Days 1-3:** watch three strangers on the current build.
- **Days 4-9:** practice moves, level 5 smoothing, stars, clock.
- **Days 10-12:** on-device log per level (attempts, failures by reason, completion, best time, days played) and a "send my results" button that hands a text summary to the share sheet. Nothing leaves the phone unless the tester sends it; no identifier. I believe the "collects nothing" statement stays accurate; check the Data safety form. Analytics waits for soft launch.
- **Days 13-14:** recruit 20 to 25 testers; ship. Google's help page (read today) says personal accounts created after 13 November 2023 need 12 testers opted in for 14 consecutive days; if that applies, use the Play closed track.

**Go** (proposed, my judgement): four of five observed players finish the practice move and level 1 unaided and can explain the rewind; half of testers reach level 10; half play on a second day unprompted. **Iterate:** the opening fails but those who pass it continue. **No-go:** people understand it and still stop before level 8 after few retries. Then the fun does not transfer and no mechanic will rescue it.
